Yes. **This time I’d approach it as a salvage-and-invention project**, not “make a box from a CPU.”

One important correction: with **no PSU and no purchased electronics**, you can make several genuinely working mechanical gadgets, but anything motorized/electronic eventually needs some source of energy or control. I won't pretend otherwise.

## First: strip the PC and keep these

You need only a screwdriver to start.

### 1. Remove the side panel

1. Put the PC on a table.
2. Remove the screws holding the side panel.
3. Slide the panel off.
4. **Do not bother with the CPU first.** Look for the drives and fans.

### 2. Remove the DVD/CD drive

1. Disconnect its cables.
2. Remove its mounting screws.
3. Slide the drive out.
4. Keep **the entire mechanism**, especially:

   * motor
   * gears
   * sliding laser carriage
   * metal rails
   * tray mechanism
   * springs
   * small screws

Optical drives really do contain precision rails, motors and a moving optical pickup.

### 3. Remove the hard drive

1. Disconnect its cable.
2. Remove its screws.
3. Take it out.
4. Keep the **whole HDD** until you've opened it carefully.
5. Inside, the most interesting parts are:

   * actuator arm
   * voice-coil motor
   * strong permanent magnets
   * spindle motor
   * platter
   * bearing

The HDD actuator is a precision mechanism built around a bearing and voice coil.

### 4. Remove the fans

1. Unplug the fan.
2. Remove its four screws.
3. Keep the fan and its cable.

A PC fan is a BLDC motor and can also be used as a generator when mechanically spun. ([Instructables][1])

# Now the genuinely interesting builds

### 🥇 1. DVD-drive **precision sliding machine**

This is my #1.

**Parts:** DVD mechanism + its own rails + motor + gears.

1. Remove the metal outer cover.
2. Find the long screw/rail carrying the laser carriage.
3. Remove the carriage carefully.
4. Keep the rail, screw and gear motor together.
5. Move the carriage manually and observe how precisely it travels.
6. Rebuild the mechanism so it moves a small object instead of the laser.
7. Use the original tray/frame as the structural base.

You have essentially salvaged a **tiny linear-motion machine** rather than just “a DVD drive.”

---

### 🥈 2. HDD **precision magnetic actuator**

Potentially the coolest component in the entire PC.

1. Open the HDD cover using its screws.
2. Locate the arm.
3. Locate the magnet surrounding the voice coil.
4. Remove the actuator assembly carefully.
5. Keep the central bearing intact.
6. Move the arm by hand and feel how freely it pivots.
7. Use the arm as a precision moving lever.

The voice-coil actuator has been repurposed experimentally for microphones and other moving mechanisms. ([Hackaday][2])

---

### 🥉 3. HDD **microphone**

This is an actual documented hack, not theoretical.

1. Salvage the HDD actuator arm and voice coil.
2. Salvage suitable wire from the PC.
3. Use the actuator so sound-induced movement produces an electrical signal.
4. Attach a lightweight diaphragm/cone made from material already available in the PC packaging/case area.
5. Connect the signal to suitable audio equipment.

A documented build successfully used the HDD arm/voice-coil actuator as the sensing element. ([Hackaday][2])

**Limitation:** it isn't a standalone microphone—you need audio equipment to hear/amplify the signal.

---

### 4. HDD **speaker**

Basically the reverse experiment.

1. Keep the actuator arm + voice coil.
2. Remove unnecessary HDD parts.
3. Attach a very light diaphragm.
4. Drive the coil with an audio signal from suitable equipment.
5. The arm's movement produces sound.

This has been demonstrated with recycled HDD actuators.

**Again:** external audio drive is required.

---

### 5. Two DVD mechanisms → **tiny XY machine**

This is where it starts becoming seriously clever.

1. Salvage two DVD laser-carriage mechanisms.
2. Put one mechanism horizontally.
3. Mount the second at 90° to the first.
4. The first gives X movement.
5. The second gives Y movement.
6. Mount a tiny object/platform on top.

You have created a miniature **two-axis positioning stage** from discarded drives. Similar DVD mechanisms have been used for experimental precision stages.

---

### 6. DVD mechanism → **automatic sliding/release mechanism**

1. Keep the DVD tray and motor together.
2. Find the mechanism that converts motor rotation into tray movement.
3. Remove unnecessary optical components.
4. Keep the tray's switches/gears.
5. Adapt the tray so its movement pushes, releases or retrieves a small object.

The important part isn't the tray—it is the **motor + gear + controlled linear movement** already engineered into the drive.

---

### 7. PC fan → **wind-powered generator**

This one can actually produce electricity.

1. Remove the fan from the PC.
2. Keep its rotor and stator intact.
3. Use the fan as a miniature wind turbine.
4. Spin it with moving air.
5. Use an LED salvaged from the computer as the load.
6. Observe whether the LED produces light.

PC fan BLDC motors have been demonstrated as small generators. ([Instructables][1])

---

### 8. HDD spindle → **hand-powered generator**

1. Open the HDD.
2. Remove the platter assembly only after identifying the spindle motor.
3. Keep the motor intact.
4. Find a way to rotate it mechanically using parts already available.
5. Connect its generated output to a suitable salvaged load.

The HDD spindle motor is a brushless motor, so it can be used experimentally as a generator.

---

### 9. HDD arm → **mechanical precision indicator**

This is unusual and potentially useful.

1. Remove the actuator arm.
2. Keep the bearing and magnet assembly.
3. Position the arm so it can freely sweep.
4. Attach a tiny pointer using salvaged lightweight material.
5. Use the pointer to indicate movement, vibration, airflow or another physical change.

Think **precision gauge**, not decoration.

---

### 10. Combine the best parts → **one machine**

This is the route I'd actually choose.

Use:

**DVD linear rail + DVD gears + HDD actuator + HDD magnets + PC switches + PC fan**

Then design **one useful machine around those mechanisms**.

For example:

**DVD rail = movement**
**HDD actuator = precision adjustment**
**HDD magnet = holding/release**
**PC switch = trigger**
**DVD gears = transmission**

That is no longer “recycling old computer parts.” You're effectively **reverse-engineering several precision mechanisms and recombining them into a new machine.**

### ⚠️ Two things not to do

Don't stare into or deliberately activate the optical-drive laser, and don't start cutting random motherboard components until you've identified them. Also be careful with the exposed HDD magnets and sharp metal chassis edges.

**My strongest three for your exact goal:**
**DVD precision mechanism → HDD actuator machine → DVD + HDD combined invention.**

The **CPU itself is actually one of the least valuable parts for this kind of invention**. The mechanisms hidden inside the drives are the gold.
